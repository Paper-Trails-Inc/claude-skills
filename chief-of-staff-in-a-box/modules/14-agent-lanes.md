# Module 14: Agent lanes

Optional. Use this when {{MY_NAME}} has a real growth or money goal and wants {{CHIEF_NAME}} to watch it, not just organize the day. A lane is a focus area {{CHIEF_NAME}} covers, with one job, a short list of sources and a clear line on what needs approval. Lanes are not new apps or new schedules. They run as sections inside the routines that already exist (module 4), so there is still one brief and one weekly review.

## The lanes

Offer these four and let the person pick. Most people want one or two.

| Lane | One job | Runs inside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Revenue** | Say whether {{COMPANY}} is on pace for its growth goal and what would move it | Weekly review, plus a monthly pace report |
| **Finance** | Keep cash and margin on target and flag drift early | A weekly money section (pick the day {{MY_NAME}} actually reads it) |
| **Relationships** | Keep promised follow ups, key partners and referral sources from going quiet | Daily brief, plus a short weekly list |
| **Personal admin** | Renewals, appointments, travel, forms and household deadlines | Daily brief and weekly preview |

Personal admin folds into {{CHIEF_NAME}}. It does not need its own agent. Keep its private items marked private (module 12).

## Interview for lanes (five questions or fewer)

1. What is the number you want to reach, by when? (example: revenue, clients, margin)
2. Where does the pipeline, revenue or cash information live today, and can Claude read it?
3. Which numbers do you check weekly, and what target should each one be compared to?
4. Who are the five to ten relationships that matter most, and how often should each hear from you?
5. What must a lane never touch or send, and who else should see its output?

If an answer is vague, ask for a real example from last month.

## What each lane does

### Revenue lane

* **Pace line.** Take the multi year goal and the current base, then show the growth rate needed each year to get there. Always show the math and call it an estimate.
* **Pipeline against plan.** New business won so far, by month, against the goal for the year. Open pipeline by stage and expected close month, with the gap to goal. Counts, amounts and stages only in the report, not names.
* **Retention and upsell.** Customers at risk (a key contact left, sentiment dropped, usage swung, a cancellation signal) and customers who may be ready for more. Draft the outreach, never send it.
* **Referral sources.** Who sends business, when they last heard from {{MY_NAME}}, and one suggested touch. A source that has gone quiet is flagged.
* **Honest caveats.** A deal amount is not the same as recurring revenue, and close dates are usually good to within a couple of weeks. Say so in the report.

### Finance lane

* **Cash.** Cash trend and anything unusual. If the business holds money for others, keep it separate from its own cash and never mix them in a total.
* **Margin against targets.** Show each margin figure beside its target and the period it covers.
* **Actuals against plan.** The largest variances only.
* **Subscription and vendor creep.** New or increased recurring charges, and renewals coming up.
* **Completeness check.** Say plainly whether the books and records were checked for completeness and balance. If a source was not readable, say "not checked." Never imply it was.
* **Out of scope.** Personal spending. No bank portals. No logging in to financial accounts. No moving money.

### Relationship lane

* **Follow up clock.** Anything {{MY_NAME}} promised that has not happened.
* **Gone quiet list.** Key people and partners past their agreed contact rhythm, with a one line draft message for each.
* **Milestones.** Dates the person told {{CHIEF_NAME}} to track, such as anniversaries and renewals.
* **Push down.** If {{MY_NAME}}'s leaders get their own chief of staff (module 11), the relationship lane for their area can be given to them, with a short monthly summary back to {{MY_NAME}}.

### Personal admin

Renewals, appointments, travel plans, forms and household deadlines, drawn only from what {{MY_NAME}} has told {{CHIEF_NAME}}. No sensitive data (module 12).

## Autonomy ladder

Every lane starts at step 1. Nothing moves up without {{MY_NAME}} saying so, one item at a time.

1. **Reports only.** Reads and reports. Writes nothing anywhere except {{CENTER_NAME}}.
2. **Drafts.** Prepares drafts of messages, tasks or updates. A person approves every one.
3. **Approved pattern.** After several weeks of drafts approved unchanged, {{MY_NAME}} may allow one narrow, low risk action to happen on its own. Example: adding a suggested task to {{CENTER_NAME}}.
4. **Never autonomous.** Sending, posting, paying, deleting, publishing, or anything that changes a customer, an account or a payment.

List the ideas for step 3 on a back burner list in {{CENTER_NAME}}. Mark each "draft" until promoted.

## Rules for every lane

1. **One job each.** If a lane needs two jobs, it is two lanes.
2. **Read only first.** Two weeks of reading and reporting before it drafts anything that touches another person.
3. **Register it.** Add each lane to the agents register (module 5) with what it reads, what it writes and what needs approval. A lane that is not on the register does not run.
4. **No new schedules.** A lane is a section in an existing routine. Add a separate scheduled task only if {{MY_NAME}} asks.
5. **Numbers need a source.** Every figure in a lane report names where it came from and the period it covers. A missing number is "missing," never estimated.
6. **Private by default.** Reports are private to {{MY_NAME}} unless {{MY_NAME}} names who else can see them.
7. **Same goal everywhere.** If the goal appears on a dashboard, the lane reads that same file, so the numbers cannot disagree.
8. **Cut what is not used.** At the 30 day noise audit (module 4), any lane section {{MY_NAME}} did not act on is shortened or retired.

## New lane checklist

Fill this in with the person before turning a lane on:

* What goal does it serve, in one line?
* What will it read, and what will it write?
* What is the worst thing it could do if it misunderstands?
* Who sees its output?
* What will {{MY_NAME}} do differently because of it? If the answer is "nothing," do not build it.

## Rollout tip

Start with the lane closest to the goal, usually revenue or finance. Run it for four weeks inside the weekly review. Fix what is noisy, then add the next lane.
